OneNGO

Feature list

What the product does today. OneNGO is a configurable management platform for NGOs: records, people, money, and receipts, plus a builder so each organisation can change how it works without a code release.

Sign-in and access

  • Sign in with a password, an email code that expires in 10 minutes, or the organisation’s identity provider. A person still has to be a member.
  • Passkeys: add one on this device, name it, and remove it from Preferences.
  • Two-step sign-in with an authenticator app. A role can require it. The app shows whether it is on or off.
  • Invitation links last 7 days and work once. Password reset links last 30 minutes and sign the person out of every device.
  • Passwords must be at least 10 characters. Short, common, predictable, or name-based passwords are refused.
  • Each organisation has its own sign-in page, /o/<code>/sign-in, with its name and logo. The last organisation on the device is remembered. “Use another organisation” forgets it.
  • An unknown code and an organisation that is not active get the same “not available” answer, so the page cannot be used to discover organisations.
  • Roles and permissions. A change to roles, permissions, or membership takes effect on the next action, and within about 5 seconds on other sessions.
  • Suspend or reactivate a person. Suspension signs them out on every device. Their profile and their name on records stay.
  • API keys act as one role. The secret is shown once. Revoking a key stops every system using it. Each key has a requests-per-minute limit.

Records

Most of the product is a generic record engine. An organisation defines the types; staff work with the records.

Fields

  • Text, long text, rich text, email, phone, number, money, date, and choice.
  • Links to other records, with the option to start a related record from the field.
  • Files and photos, with type and size limits. Location from the device or typed coordinates. Signature on a line.
  • Calculated fields, fields copied from a related record, and fields that show or lock based on the record’s state.
  • Sensitive fields, such as a PAN, stored encrypted. People without permission see them masked. Unique sensitive values stay unique without being decrypted for the check.

Working with records

  • Lists grouped by the organisation’s record groups, with search and filters for status, dates, amounts, and other fields.
  • Cards on a phone, cards or table on a larger screen. “Assigned to me” and “Shared with me”.
  • Create, edit, and cancel an edit. The form names any required field still empty.
  • Share a record with a person or a role, as view or edit, with an optional end date.
  • Pin a list to your own Home, or to a draft of everyone’s Home that appears after the page is published.
  • Select rows, or every record matching the current filters, then edit fields or share them together. Each row goes through the same checks as a single edit.
  • Import a CSV of at most 10 MB and 20,000 rows. Match columns, choose create or update, skip problem rows, and download the failed rows.
  • Export the current list. A large export runs in the background and arrives as a notification.
  • Change history on each record, and an organisation audit log filtered by person, record type, date, or area.

Workflows and automation

  • A record moves through named steps. A step can require fields and can refuse itself with a message, such as a payment date in the future.
  • A step that needs approval waits in Inbox until enough different people approve it. The requester cannot approve their own request.
  • Inbox has Approvals and Notifications. The number on Inbox is the approvals waiting, and it refreshes about once a minute.
  • Approve, or reject with an optional reason. If the record changed while the request was open, the decision has to be made again.
  • Record actions, such as a reminder or a receipt, appear when the role allows them and the record is in a state that offers them. The same actions can run from a list.
  • Automations start when a record changes, using message templates.
  • Background jobs for imports, bulk changes, and rebuilds: Waiting, Running, Done, Failed, or Cancelled. A failed job shows the failed rows. A running job can be cancelled.

Money and receipts

  • A double-entry ledger. A journal that does not balance is refused. An account balance changes only through a recorded transaction.
  • Posting rules say which record and status posts to which accounts. A module that handles money uses these rules rather than its own ledger.
  • Each organisation chooses the month its financial year starts. April is the default. A year that starts in January is named 2026; any other start is named 2026-27.
  • Entries book into the open year that contains the payment date. A closed year cannot be changed except by reopening it with a recorded reason. The start month locks once a year is opened or a receipt is issued.
  • Receipt series with a prefix, a format, and a reset. Numbers restart each financial year, stay gap-free, and are never reused. A void keeps the number. A replacement gets a new linked number.
  • Each receipt keeps the organisation’s legal name, display name, address, contact details, registrations in force that day, logo, and authorised signatory. Later changes do not rewrite it.
  • Print a receipt. Anyone who can print can copy a public link. The donor opens it without an account. Revoking the link stops that address. A new link uses a different address.
  • UPI is the default way to take a payment: a payment stays pending until it is matched or confirmed. Server only Razorpay and Cashfree can be saved per organisation, with secrets encrypted and never shown again. There is no gateway setup screen yet.

Donations module

The one module that ships with the product. When it is enabled, Fundraising appears for people whose role includes it. Finance can work the records. Viewer can read them.

  • Donors, and donations numbered per financial year, such as DON/2026-27/00001.
  • Choosing a donor copies their name, email, phone, and PAN. Those copies stay editable when no donor is chosen.
  • Workflow: Raised, then Paid or Cancelled. Mark as paid asks for the method and the payment date, which cannot be in the future.
  • Methods: UPI, cash, bank transfer, cheque, and online gateway. A paid UPI donation needs a transaction reference.
  • Amount, processing fee, and a calculated net amount. The fee cannot be more than the donation.
  • Pay now creates a UPI payment and, once paid, marks the donation paid.
  • A paid donation posts to Bank or Cash and to Donations received, and issues a numbered donation acknowledgement.
  • Thank-you and reminder messages, a send-reminder action, a donation summary, and a donation register.

Organisation setup

  • An operator brings an approved NGO on from one request: profile, administrator role, chosen modules, financial year, receipt prefix, and the first administrator. The request is validated before anything is written. The same request can be retried without creating a second organisation.
  • Identity: display name, legal name, contact email, phone, website, address, time zone, and default language. The organisation code and sign-in link are shown and cannot be edited here.
  • Registrations such as 80G, 12A, and FCRA, with number, authority, issue date, and expiry. The same type and number cannot be entered twice. Numbers are stored in capitals.
  • Logo: PNG, JPEG, or WebP, at most 1 MB. It appears in the app, on the sign-in page, and on new receipts.
  • Authorised signatory: a name, a designation, and a PNG signature of at most 200 kB. A receipt keeps the signatory it was issued with. Each receipt rule has its own signatories, and a rule with none issues no receipt and shows an error.
  • Setup checklist: confirm identity, add a registration, confirm the open financial year, review the receipt series, and give someone besides the administrator a working role. Email and online payments are optional.
  • Storage quota in whole gigabytes, and the API request limit per key.
  • Lifecycle, done by an operator: Active, Suspended, or Closed. Suspension ends sessions and stops API keys. Closing is permanent and keeps the data. Nothing here deletes records.

Builder

Administrators with the setup permission define how the organisation works. Changes stay drafts until published. History can restore an older published version as the newest one. Publishing does not alter historical records. Editing a definition needs a larger screen. Browsing works on a phone.

  • Record types: fields, numbering, and rules.
  • Workflows, including approvals.
  • List views: columns, filters, and layout.
  • Record groups under Records.
  • Pages for Home and the extra pages under More.
  • Actions, such as a reminder or a receipt.
  • Automations and message templates.
  • Posting rules and receipt rules.
  • Scripts and the organisation theme and layout.
  • Translations beyond the shipped languages.
  • Roles, and an access map of who can reach which record types and actions.
  • Modules: enable a shipped package such as Donations, which brings its record types, roles, accounts, and receipt series together.
  • A definition that came with a module keeps the organisation’s edits as a patch, so a later product update can still arrive.
  • Data migration under Administration: load CSV exports of an earlier system onto record types and keep each old id against the record it became.

Override scripts

When configuration is not enough, a script author adds logic at defined moments. A script never acts on its own. It proposes changes, and the platform checks them and applies them in the same save, or not at all.

  • Moments: before save, validate, after save, workflow guard, and custom button.
  • A proposal can be a balanced journal, a new record, a change to a related record, a notification, or a call to an approved webhook.
  • Limits: 20 proposals per run, 50 per save, and five levels of nested saves. Time and memory are limited.
  • Journals must balance to the paisa, use only declared accounts, fall in an open year, and stay within the script’s per-run maximum.
  • A script cannot issue or change receipts, reverse entries, open or close a year, or delete records.
  • Try a script against sample data. The trial changes nothing. Server only The Builder screen shows field changes. Proposed actions are returned by the server and are not shown on that screen yet.
  • Publish and roll back. A finance script’s publish and rollback are written to the change history and the administrators are notified.
  • A script that acts as a role or reads ledger balances can be published only by an organisation administrator.
  • Described, not built Calculated fields, scheduled jobs, report shaping, and payment matching are not script moments yet.

Connectors

  • SMS, WhatsApp, and webhook connectors. Nothing is sent until a connector is set up and switched on.
  • Secrets are entered once and later shown as Set. They are replaced, not read back.
  • Deliveries are listed as retrying, delivered, or failed. A failed delivery can be retried.
  • Email goes through the organisation’s connector, through OneNGO’s email service, or not at all until a connector exists.
  • Server only Named fixed recipients, such as an external auditor, can be kept in settings so a script notifies them by name and never by address. The settings screen has no editor for them yet.

Privacy

  • Server only Consent for a donor, beneficiary, or member: a purpose granted or withdrawn, with the source and who recorded it. History is append-only. There is no screen yet.
  • Server only Erasure of a suspended person replaces personal details with a pseudonym and keeps amounts, dates, receipt numbers, and the books. The erasure rules are in the software and still await product-owner approval. There is no screen yet.
  • Financial records are not deleted by erasure. The default retention period is 8 years after the record’s date, pending counsel confirmation. Nothing yet deletes them when that period ends.
  • A posted journal description can still contain a donor’s name, because a posted entry cannot be rewritten. Payment-provider payloads are kept as received.

People and language

  • My profile opens from the account card. Name and email are changed by an administrator. The person edits only the profile fields the organisation marked as theirs.
  • A read-only People directory of active colleagues, including the profile fields marked for colleagues. Invited and suspended people stay on the administration list.
  • Sensitive directory fields are masked unless the viewer may see them.
  • Languages: English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, and Marathi. The choice is personal. The organisation default applies to new people and to messages. Missing translations fall back to English.
  • About this app shows the version. Sign out ends the session on this device only.

App experience

  • Bottom bar on a phone, side navigation on a larger screen, and a breadcrumb trail on the larger screen.
  • The bar shows OneNGO with the organisation’s display name and logo. Browser titles read “screen | OneNGO”.
  • The app can be installed. What is already on screen stays there when the connection drops. Changes wait until the connection returns. A new version asks the person to reload.
  • Public pages, public forms, and public record views for people who are not signed in.
  • Print views for receipts and other documents.
  • No fonts, images, scripts, or styles are loaded from the public internet.

Operator console

One NGO Console is a separate application. It does not share a sign-in with members. Two-factor authentication is required on every sign-in, and again before suspend or close.

  • Find an organisation by its code, or an onboarding request by its id.
  • Validate an onboarding request, then provision it. Editing the request asks for validation again.
  • Retry failed onboarding from the last finished step. It does not start a second organisation.
  • Suspend an active organisation, reactivate a suspended one, or close a suspended one. Suspend and close need a reason of at most 500 characters.
  • Manage operator accounts. Each action appears only when that operator’s account includes it.
  • Platform activity: who did what, and when.

Not in the product yet

These appear in the product brief. They are not modules in this repository, and they are not screens in the apps.

Named for the first delivery, not found here

  • Volunteer management: sign-up, skills, availability, shifts, hours, and certificates.
  • Compliance calendar: registration renewals, document expiry, and reminders.

Later modules

  • Projects, expenses, earnings, invoices, accounts and transfers, physical assets, meetings, and requests.
  • Donor journeys, pledges, grants, beneficiary case management, impact reporting, events, membership fees, governance, in-kind donations, and bulk email.
  • Paid WhatsApp and SMS business messaging, and an outbound event feed.

Out of scope for now

  • Tax-credit receipts, FCRA books, filing with regulators, and e-signing Form 10B or 10BB.
  • Payroll and human-resources management.
  • A native mobile app. The installable web app is the field client.
  • Self-registration by an NGO, subscription billing, and deleting an organisation’s data when it is closed.
