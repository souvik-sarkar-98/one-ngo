Start here

OneNGO keeps an organisation’s records, people, and money in one place. What you can open depends on the role you were given. Record names, pages, and workflow steps are set by that organisation, so the lists you see may differ from the examples here.

One organisation at a time

Sign in with the organisation code from your invitation or administrator. OneNGO does not publish a list of organisations.

Sign in

Open the link your administrator sent. It looks like the organisation’s own page, with the organisation’s name and logo when those have been set. You can also start from the general sign-in page and type the organisation code yourself.

With a password

  1. Stay on the Password tab.
  2. Enter the email address on your invitation and your password.
  3. Choose Sign in.

With an email code

  1. Open the Email code tab.
  2. Enter your email and choose Email me a code.
  3. Enter the code from the message. It expires in 10 minutes.
  4. Choose Sign in.

The page always says a code is on its way if the address might belong to a member. That wording does not confirm whether the address is registered.

With your organisation’s identity provider

If the organisation has connected a provider, the sign-in page shows Sign in with that provider. You still need to be an existing member. A role that requires two-step sign-in still asks for the authenticator code afterwards. That extra step stays available for 5 minutes.

Forgot your password

  1. On the Password tab, choose Forgot password?
  2. Enter your email and choose Email me a reset link.
  3. Open the link. It works once and expires in 30 minutes.
  4. Choose a new password of at least 10 characters, type it again, and save it.
  5. Sign in with the new password. Saving it signs you out of every device.

A short sentence you can remember is easier to keep and harder to guess than a short jumble. The organisation rejects passwords that are too short, too common, too predictable, or built from your name or the organisation’s name.

First visit from an invitation

  1. Open the invitation link. It is valid for 7 days and works once.
  2. Set a password, using the same rules as a reset.
  3. Go to sign in and use that password.
Two-step sign-in

If your role requires an authenticator and it is not set up, sign-in stops and tells you to ask an administrator. The app shows whether two-step is On or Off under More. It does not include a screen for scanning a setup code. When two-step is already on, sign-in asks for the 6-digit code from your authenticator app.

Choose Use another organisation when you need a different organisation code. The general sign-in page remembers the last organisation unless you do that.

Move around

After sign-in, the bar along the bottom of a phone, or the side on a larger screen, has the places your role allows. The same four places appear on every size.

PlaceUse it to
HomeSee the organisation’s home page and the lists you have pinned.
RecordsOpen a record type, search, and create or update records.
InboxDecide approvals and read notifications. A number appears when approvals are waiting.

A back arrow returns you from a list or a settings page to the place you came from. On a larger screen, a breadcrumb line under the top bar shows the full trail, such as Records › Fundraising › Donations. Each part of the trail is a link back to that place.

The app can be installed on a phone. If you are offline, what is already on screen stays there. Changes cannot be saved until the connection returns. When a new version is ready, the app asks you to reload.

Home

Home greets you by name and shows the pages and figures your role is allowed to see. Each record group appears as a card with its record types and a quick New button. If you have no access to records yet, Home says so and asks you to get a role from an administrator.

Pin a list

  1. Open the record list you use often.
  2. Choose Pin to my Home.
  3. Return to Home. Rename the pin, move it up or down, or unpin it from there.

Pin to everyone’s Home adds the list to a draft of the shared home page. It appears for other people only after someone publishes that page in the Builder.

Records

Records lists the record groups, one under another, and the record types in each. Each group header can be collapsed, and the app remembers that choice. Types that are not in a group appear under Other. When there are many types, a search box at the top finds one by name.

Find a record

  1. Choose the record type.
  2. Search the list. The search starts a moment after you stop typing.
  3. Open Filters and set status, dates, amounts, or other fields the list offers. Choose Reset to clear them, or Apply to see the result.
  4. On a larger screen, switch between Cards and Table. Phones always use cards.
  5. Open a row to see the record. Use the ⋮ menu on a row for Edit and the steps that row can take.

Chips at the top show status views of the list, such as Mine or Outstanding. Sort cycles a column from ascending to descending to no sort; the arrow shows the current direction. Each filter you apply appears as a pill you can remove on its own. As you scroll, the next page loads. Keyboard users and people who reduce motion get a Load more button instead. If nothing matches, the list says so and offers to clear the filters.

Assigned to me and Shared with me narrow the list to work that involves you.

Open a record

A record always opens in a panel over the list, on every size, so you return to the same place when you close it. The panel shows the record’s sections, each of which can be collapsed. Records of another type that point at this one, such as a donor’s donations, appear as related sections showing the latest ten, the count, and sometimes a total.

If a step is waiting for approval, the panel shows it at the top with Approve and Reject. The buttons for the steps you can take are at the bottom, followed by Edit.

Create or edit

  1. Choose New, and complete the required fields. The form names any field you still need. Some forms have more than one step, shown as Step 1 of 2.
  2. Save. The record is created, or your changes are saved. A new record appears at the top of the list.
  3. To change an existing record, choose Edit, then Save changes. Cancel edit leaves it as it was.

Some fields are calculated, copied, or read-only. Fields that are locked in the record’s current state are read-only. Some fields are hidden from your role; they show *** in place of the value, and you cannot filter, sort, or total by them.

Field types you will meet: a link field lets you pick another record or start a related one. File fields accept a drag, a chosen file, or a photo, and reject a type or size the field does not allow. Location fields can use your device’s location or coordinates you type. Signature fields take a signature on the line. Rich text fields offer bold, lists, headings, quotes, and web or email links.

Choose a related record

A reference field, such as a donation’s donor, opens a record picker. Type a few letters to search the record type. Up to eight matches appear, with an email or phone line to tell similar names apart. If the person or item does not exist yet, choose New with the text you typed, and it is created and selected. The ✕ clears your choice.

Where the Donations module is on, choosing a donor fills in the donor’s name, email, phone, and PAN on the donation and locks those fields. The details are copied when you save. Changing the donor copies the new donor’s details; a donation keeps the details it had when you did not change the donor. With no donor chosen, you can type the details yourself.

Move a record through its workflow

  1. Open the record.
  2. Fill in what the step asks for. The step also asks for any field that the state it moves to needs.
  3. Choose the step name.

If the step needs approval, it is sent to Inbox and waits until enough different people approve it. You cannot approve your own request. If the record changes while the request is open, refresh and try again.

Receipts

A step that issues a receipt tells you the receipt number. Open Receipts on the record, then Print. A voided receipt shows the reason. Anyone who can print can copy a public link. The donor opens that address without an account and sees the same receipt as Print, including the donor name and the signatory kept with that receipt. Revoke public link stops that address. A new link uses a different address.

Other actions on the record, such as sending a reminder, appear when your role allows them and the record is in a state that offers them. The ⋮ menu on a list can run the same actions.

Share a record

  1. Open the record and choose Share.
  2. Type a person’s name, email, or a role.
  3. Choose Can view or Can edit.
  4. Leave Ends on empty, or pick today or a later day. The share ends after that day.
  5. Choose Share. Remove a person or role from the same panel when the share should stop.

Import and export

  1. On the list, choose Import.
  2. Upload a CSV file of at most 10 MB and 20,000 rows, or download the sample file for this list’s columns.
  3. Match each column to a field, or leave it as Not imported. Match at least one column.
  4. Choose whether a matching field updates an existing record, or whether every row creates a new one.
  5. Check the counts for create, update, and problems. Rows with problems are skipped.
  6. Decide whether automations should run, then start the import.

Change many records

  1. Select the rows, or select every record that matches the current filters.
  2. Choose Edit fields or Share.
  3. Fill in only the fields that should change, or name who to share with.
  4. Apply. Each record goes through the same checks as a single edit.

You can close the panel. A notification arrives when the job finishes. Some rows may be done, waiting for approval, or failed. Stop ends the job before the last row.

Inbox

Inbox has two lists: Approvals and Notifications. It opens on Approvals when anything is waiting for you, and on Notifications otherwise. The number on Inbox is the approvals waiting for you. It refreshes when you move around the app and about once a minute while the app is open.

Decide an approval

  1. Open Approvals. Search, sort, or filter by type, step, or who requested it if the list is long.
  2. Read the record, the change from one status to another, who requested it, how long it has waited, and how many approvals are still needed.
  3. Open the record if you need the detail. The request is shown at the top of the record, so you can check the whole record before deciding.
  4. Choose Approve, or Reject. Reject opens a small panel where you can add a reason, if you want to.

Your approval is recorded immediately. The step is applied when the request has enough distinct approvals. Decided requests leave the list. If someone else has already changed the request, refresh and decide again.

Notifications

The newest notifications come first. A new one is marked New. Tapping a notification marks it read and opens its record. Read ones are dimmed. Mark all read clears the lot at once. A finished import, export, or bulk change can open from its notification.

Your account

More shows your name, email, and organisation. Choose your card to open My profile. Your name and email are changed by an administrator. Other profile fields appear when the organisation has set them, and you can save the ones you are allowed to edit. If none are editable, the profile says there is nothing there you can change.

Language

Under Settings, open Language and choose English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, or Marathi. The choice is yours. The organisation’s default language applies to new people and to messages until someone chooses their own. Screen text falls back to English where a translation is not published yet.

Two-step sign-in on the same list shows On or Off. About this app shows the version you are using. Sign out is on your initials at the top of the screen. It ends the session on this device, and you will need to sign in again here. It does not sign out your other devices.

Passkeys

When your organisation has turned passkeys on, a passkey lets this device confirm you with its fingerprint, face, or PIN. The device keeps those. The password and email code stay available.

  1. Sign in as usual, then open Settings and choose Save a passkey on this device. The option appears only when your browser can use one.
  2. Give it a name if you like, then confirm with the device. You can keep up to 10. If two-step sign-in is on, the app asks for your password and a current code first.
  3. Next time, on the organisation’s sign-in page, choose Sign in with this device. If two-step sign-in is on, you still enter the code.

Remove a passkey from Settings. That ends your passkey sign-ins for this organisation. A passkey cannot be your first way in; sign in another way first.

If something will not open

  • This could not be loaded, or you are offline: check the connection and try again. Unsaved edits wait until you are back online.
  • You do not have access: your role does not include that record, page, or area. Ask an administrator.
  • A link says it is incomplete, expired, or already used: ask for a new invitation or reset link.
  • The organisation is not available for sign-in: the code is wrong, or an operator has suspended or closed the organisation.
  • Your role requires two-step sign-in: ask an administrator. There is no setup screen in the app.

People and invitations

Administration, then People, is the only people list. It shows names, roles, contact details, status, two-step, and dates. Filter by Active, Invited (added but never signed in), Suspended, or All.

Add a person

  1. Choose Add a person.
  2. Enter their name and email, and choose at least one role.
  3. Leave Send the invitation email now selected when they should set a password immediately.
  4. Choose Add and invite.

The invitation link is valid for 7 days. Resend invitation replaces an unused link. A suspended person must be reactivated before a new invitation can be sent.

Change access

  • Change roles on one person, or select several and add or remove a role for all of them.
  • Suspend signs that person out on every device. They cannot sign in until you reactivate them. Their profile stays, read-only, and their name stays on records, approvals, and the audit log.
  • Reactivate lets them sign in again. It does not restore a session that suspension ended.

The Two-step column shows Required · not set when a role demands an authenticator and the person has not got one. They cannot finish sign-in until that is resolved. The app does not provide an enrolment screen for them or for you.

Roles

Open a role to see what it allows and who has it. Add active or invited people from that page. Removing someone takes that role away at once. A role marked Requires two-step sign-in tells you that people without an authenticator will be asked about it the next time they sign in. In practice, sign-in stops and tells them to ask an administrator, because there is no setup screen.

Connectors

Nothing is sent until a connector is set up and switched on. The list shows the kind, what uses it, and the last delivery.

  1. Choose New connector, or open an existing one.

Deliveries are listed as retrying, delivered, or failed. Retry now sends a failed delivery again.

Jobs and the audit log

Background jobs lists imports, bulk changes, and rebuilds for the whole organisation. A job is Waiting, Running, Done, Failed, or Cancelled. Open a failed job to see the failed rows. Cancel job stops one that is still going.

The audit log answers who did what, in which area, and when. Filter by person, record type, date, or area. Areas include records, people, roles, setup, connectors, API keys, money, files, and the organisation. Use it when a receipt, a role change, or a setting needs to be traced. It is a record of what happened, not a place to undo it.

Organisation settings

Identity, registrations, and logo

  • The organisation code and the sign-in link are shown so you can send people to the right page. The code itself is not edited here.
  • Legal name, contact email, phone, website, address, and time zone are the details printed and used for the organisation. Save identity after a change, then confirm it on the checklist when you are ready.
  • The country is the two-letter code, such as IN. A website starts with https://.
  • A registration needs a type and a number. Issue and expiry dates are optional, and the expiry cannot be earlier than the issue date. The same type and number cannot be entered twice.
  • The logo is a PNG, JPEG, or WebP image of at most 1 MB. It appears in the app, on the sign-in page, and on new receipts.
  • The authorised signatory is a name, a designation, and a PNG signature of at most 200 kB. An administrator uploads it under Organisation settings. A receipt keeps the signatory it was issued with. Receipts issued before a signatory was saved keep a blank line. Each receipt rule prints only the signatories chosen for it under Builder, Receipt rules, Signatories, and there is no shared default. A rule with none chosen cannot issue a receipt: the step that would issue one stops with a message, and no receipt is numbered.

Language, storage, API, and the financial year

  • Display name is the name people see in the app.
  • Default language applies to new people and to messages. Each person can still choose their own under More.
  • Storage shows how much of the quota is used. The quota is a whole number of gigabytes.
  • API requests per minute is the limit for each API key.
  • Financial year starts in sets when books, receipt numbers, and record numbers begin a new year. After a year is opened or a receipt is issued, that month stays fixed so closed years and receipt numbers do not move.

Moving donations and expenses from your old system

This part shows how to bring donations and expense claims from your old system into OneNGO, using Administration › Data › Migration. It is written for the person doing the migration. Read all of it before you start, and work through the steps in order. (The design behind the migrator is in docs/documents.html#architecture, Appendix E.)

1. PrepareExport CSV files and tidy the values (Steps 1 and 2).
2. ConfigureMap columns, set the load order, add bills and opening balances (Steps 3 to 6).
3. CheckSafe to repeat as often as you like. Nothing is saved (Step 7).
4. Run and verifyFinal. The migration becomes read-only (Steps 8 and 9).

What the migration does

  • Each donation and each expense claim becomes a record in OneNGO with its own new number, for example DON/2026-27/00001 or EXP/2026-27/00001.
  • A paid donation or paid expense claim is posted to the books at the date it was paid. Donations credit Donations received. Expenses debit the matching expense account (Administration, Programme, Travel or Other).
  • Unpaid items (raised donations, and claims that are draft, submitted, approved or not approved) are loaded as records only. They do not post.
  • Your old receipt numbers are not re-issued. OneNGO does not print new receipts for migrated donations.
  • Your old system's numbers are not kept as the record number. Keep the old number in a field you can search, such as Notes.
  • Only the current financial year posts to the books. Older records are handled through the opening balances (Step 6).

Rules for the values:

  • Dates: dd/mm/yyyy (15/04/2026) or yyyy-mm-dd (2026-04-15). Any other format is refused.
  • Amounts: plain numbers in rupees, such as 1000 or 2400.50.

  • Paid From: must be the exact account code BANK or CASH. If your old system says something else (for example Main bank), add a value rule in the Map step (Step 3).
  • Status: must match one of the statuses in the mapping tables. Anything else is refused.

Step 4: Set the load order

On the Order step, put the donations file above the expenses file. Files load from top to bottom, and Check follows the same order. If expenses are paid from cash that donations bring in, the donations must load first.

Step 5: Attach bills (optional)

On the Files step, under Attachments, upload the bill images or PDFs. Each expense claim's Bill field takes the file name as it appears in your upload, such as scans/E-201.pdf. Each attachment can be used by one row only, and a bill must be within 5 MB.

Step 6: Books (opening balances)

On the Books step, enter the balances you had at the start of the current financial year. This is needed whenever a payment could draw on money from earlier years.

  • Cutover date: the first day of the current financial year, for example 01/04/2026. Any other date is refused.
  • Opening journal: one line per account. Debit the bank and cash accounts with their balances and credit OPENING_FUNDS for the total. Debits and credits must be equal, or the books are refused.
  • Receipt numbering: the next number for each receipt series, for example donation-receipt → 2046 if the highest receipt number used so far is 2045.

The books apply before any row loads, which is why a paid expense can be paid from the opening cash.

Step 7: Check

Select Check. Nothing is saved at this step. Check runs every row through the same rules as a normal save, in the load order, and predicts the journal entries each row will post. For each file it shows how many rows are ready, how many have problems, and how many records will be created or updated. Problems show the file, the row number and the message; row 1 is the header, so the first data row is row 2. Fix the CSV files, upload them again, and select Check again until no problems remain. Check is safe to repeat as often as you like.

Step 8: Run

When Check shows no problems, select Run N rows. The migration posts the books first, then loads each row in its own transaction. A row that fails is left out and listed; the others still load. You can leave the page; a notice arrives in your inbox when the run finishes. Automations, mail and receipts stay off during the run, so no thank-you letters or receipts are sent. If some rows failed, select Download failed rows for that file, correct them in your CSV and load them in a new migration. A finished migration is read-only, so you cannot change its files.

Step 9: Check the result

  1. Counts: the donation and expense records should match the created counts in the Run step, plus any rows you knowingly left out.
  2. Money: open the bank and cash statements. Paid donations and paid claims should appear at their paid dates.
  3. Reports: the donation register and expense register for the current year should match your old system's totals for those items.
  4. Receipts: no new receipts were issued. Open a migrated donation and check that Notes shows its old receipt number.

If a total does not match, do not change records by hand. Note the difference and talk to your administrator so the cause can be found first.

What the migration does not do

  • It does not post donation processing fees. The journal uses the full donation amount.
  • It does not migrate paid items from earlier financial years as journal entries. Enter their totals as opening balances.
  • It does not keep old system numbers as OneNGO record numbers. Keep them in Notes or Payment reference.
  • Migrated claims skip the approval step and the transition checks, such as the bill being attached before submission. Claims you create in OneNGO still need approval in the normal way.

What the Builder is

OneNGO has no fixed screens for "donations" or "volunteers". Everything people see is described by settings called definitions, and the Builder is where you create and change them. For example:

  • a record type says what information you keep, such as a donation with an amount and a donor;
  • a workflow says the stages it goes through, such as Raised, then Paid;
  • a list says how people browse those records;
  • a message template says what email goes out.

You build these up like blocks. When you are happy, you publish, and everyone sees the change straight away. Nothing you type affects anyone until you publish it.

Your first set-up, step by step

1. Record typeWhat you keep. Publish this first.
2. Workflow, list, actionsHow it moves, how people browse it, what buttons it has.
3. Messages and automationsTemplates first, then the automation that sends them.
4. AccessGive each role the permissions it needs.
5. LayoutPut it in the navigation so people can find it.

This example builds a small "Volunteer shift" register: people sign up for shifts, a coordinator approves them, and the volunteer gets an email. Every Builder module is made the same way.

  1. Create the record type. In the Builder, open Record types and press New record type. Type the Name "Volunteer shift" and press Create.
  2. Add the fields. Press Add field for each box you need: Volunteer (Person), Shift date (Date), Hours (Number), Notes (Long text). Tick Required where a value must be given. Choose a Title field so each record has a readable name.
  3. Publish the record type. Press Check, fix anything it lists, then Publish… and Publish. Other definitions can only point to a record type once it is published.
  4. Add a workflow. Open Workflows, press New workflow, and choose the record type "Volunteer shift". Add the statuses Requested, Approved and Done, and steps such as "Approve" (from Requested to Approved). Under Approval, tick Needs approval if a coordinator must agree. Publish.
  5. Add a list. Open Lists, press New list, and pick the record type. Add chips (for example "Mine" and "Requested"), choose the table columns, and build the create form. Watch the preview on the right. Publish.
  6. Write the message. Open Message templates, press New message template, and write a Subject and Message using placeholders such as {{ record.shiftDate }}. Use Send a test to me. Publish.
  7. Send it automatically. Open Automations, press New automation, and set the Event to "The status changes" and To status to "approved". Add an action "Notify people", pick the template, and send to the volunteer. Publish.
  8. Give people access. In Access (or Administration › Roles), give the volunteer role "See" and "Create" on Volunteer shifts, and the coordinator role the "Approve" step.
  9. Put it in the menu. Open Layout, use Add a record to add Volunteer shifts to the navigation bar, then Publish layout.
TipPublish things in the order they depend on each other: the record type first, then the workflow, list, templates and automations that use it.

Formula boxes

Many settings take a short formula, for example "Only when", "Visible when", "Calculated as" or "Must be true". A formula is like a spreadsheet formula. It reads the record's fields and gives back a value, often true or false.

Examples

record.amount > 0                      the amount is more than zero
record.amount >= 50000                 fifty thousand rupees or more
record.status == 'raised'              the record is in the Raised status

Console sign-in

Operators use a separate console, One NGO Console. It is not the organisation app, and it does not share a sign-in with members. Two-factor authentication is required on every sign-in.

  1. Open the console address your installation gave you.
  2. Choose Continue to identity provider.
  3. Complete that provider’s sign-in, including a fresh two-factor check.
What you are allowed to do

The console shows an action only when your operator account includes it. You may be able to look up an organisation without being able to suspend or close it. Sign out ends the operator session.

Find an organisation

  1. Open Find.
  2. Enter the organisation code, or switch the list to Request ID and enter the onboarding request id.
  3. Choose Find.

Organisation status shows the organisation, the onboarding request, and recent history. There is no such organisation or onboarding request when neither matches. A request that has not created an organisation yet can still be opened by its request id.

Onboard an organisation

Onboarding creates one organisation from one approved request: its profile, the administrator role, the modules you name, the financial year, and the first administrator. The administrator receives an invitation to set a password. Validate the request before anything is written. Provision stays unavailable until validation succeeds. Editing the text asks you to validate again.

  1. Open Onboard.
  2. Paste the request. Use a new request id for a new organisation.
  3. Choose Validate. Fix every field the page reports.
  4. Choose Provision.
  5. Copy the organisation code and the request id from the result.
  6. Open Find with that code and confirm the organisation is active and the invitation is sent.

The same request id with the same contents resumes a request that stopped, or returns the organisation it already made. The same request id with different contents is refused. Do not invent a second id for a retry of the same approved request.

PasteOne approved request, with a new request id.

What to put in the request

  • requestId: 3 to 100 letters, digits, or . _ : -, starting with a letter or digit.
  • organisation.code: 3 to 40 characters, lowercase letters, digits, and single dashes, starting with a letter. These codes are reserved: admin, api, app, internal, onengo, operator, sign-in, support, www.
  • Display name, legal name, contact email, and a full address. Country is a two-letter code. Time zone is a name such as Asia/Kolkata.
  • defaultLanguage: en, hi, ta, te, bn, or mr.
  • region: the hosting region for this installation, such as asia-south1.
  • modules: module keys this installation ships and has published. The list may be empty. A module that depends on another must include that dependency. donations is the donations package when this installation publishes it.
  • financialYear.startMonth: 1 for January through 12. current is a year such as 2026 when the year starts in January, or 2026-27 when it starts in a later month.
  • receiptPrefix: optional. Use it only when one of the modules issues receipts. 1 to 12 capital letters, digits, or dashes, starting with a letter or digit.
  • Registrations are optional at this step and limited to 20. The NGO administrator confirms them during setup.

Retry a request that stopped

  1. Find the organisation by request id.
  2. If your account may retry, choose Retry failed work.
  3. Read the status again. The request continues from the last step it finished. It does not start a second organisation.

Suspend, reactivate, or close

Open the organisation by its code. Lifecycle actions appear only when your account includes them. None of these actions deletes the organisation’s records.

Enter the reason after the extra sign-in

The first click sends you to the identity provider for a fresh check. When you return, type the reason and click the same action again. The reason is not kept across that check. Suspend and close require a reason of at most 500 characters. The reason is stored in the audit.

Suspend

Only an active organisation can be suspended. Suspension signs every member out, stops the organisation’s API keys, and blocks sign-in. Tell the NGO administrator before you do it, unless the reason is an incident that cannot wait.

Reactivate

Only a suspended organisation can be reactivated. Members can sign in again. Sessions that ended, and API keys that were stopped, are not restored. The administrator creates new keys if a system should connect again.

Close

Close is permanent. A closed organisation does not open again. Suspend it first. An organisation whose provisioning failed can also be closed. Closing keeps every row for export and retention. It removes the organisation from sign-in.
