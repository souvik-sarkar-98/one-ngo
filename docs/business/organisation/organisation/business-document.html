Organisation identity, onboarding and lifecycle

Domain: organisation · Entity: organisation

Implementation-independent · reconciled against backend + frontend

1. Purpose

OneNGO is one product used by many NGOs. Each NGO is an organisation with its own identity: the name people know it by, its legal name, how to reach it, its registered address, its registrations (trust or society registration, 12A, 80G, FCRA and so on) and its logo. That identity appears in the application, on the organisation's sign-in page and on the documents it issues. A OneNGO operator brings an approved NGO onto the platform; the NGO's primary administrator then finishes setting it up. An operator can later suspend, reactivate or close an organisation without losing its data. Every receipt keeps the issuer identity that applied when it was issued, so later changes never rewrite an old receipt.

2. Actors

  • OneNGO operator — an authorised person, named in the environment where the operator command runs. Onboards an approved request, checks a request without onboarding it, inspects an organisation's status and history, retries a failed onboarding, and suspends, reactivates or closes an organisation. Operators act outside every organisation; nothing an organisation's own people or API keys hold lets them do any of this.
  • Primary administrator — named in the onboarding request. Receives the invitation to set a password, holds the Organisation administrator role, and finishes setup.
  • Organisation administrator (organisation-settings permission) — maintains the display and legal names, contact details, address, time zone, registrations and logo, and confirms setup steps. Cannot change the organisation code, hosting region or lifecycle status.
  • Any member — sees the organisation's display name and logo in the application.
  • Anyone, before signing in — opening an organisation's sign-in link sees only its display name, logo and sign-in options, and only while the organisation is active.

4. Business rules

Identity

  • The display name and the legal name are separate and both required once onboarded. The product name OneNGO is fixed and is not part of an organisation's identity.
  • A registration's number is stored trimmed and in capitals, so aaatc1234f and AAATC1234F are the same number; an organisation cannot hold the same type and number twice. Two different organisations may hold the same registration.
  • An expiry date cannot be before the issue date.
  • The organisation code, hosting region and lifecycle status belong to the operator. Organisation administrators see the code but cannot change it.
  • Every change to the identity, registrations, logo or setup confirmation is recorded in the organisation's audit log.

Logo

  • The logo must be a PNG, JPEG or WebP image of at most 1 MB. Other images (including SVG) and other files are refused.
  • The current logo, and any earlier logo kept by an issued receipt, is never removed by the clean-up of unused uploads. An earlier logo no receipt keeps is cleaned up like any unused upload.
  • Members only ever see their own organisation's logo. Before sign-in, only an active organisation's current logo is shown, from the platform itself; no logo is ever loaded from another website.

Onboarding

  • Only an operator onboards an organisation, from a request file, never from an organisation's own screens or credentials. The request never contains a password.
  • The whole request is checked before anything is written, and every problem is reported against the field it concerns. Unknown properties, an unknown or unpublished module, a module whose prerequisite is missing, an unsupported language, an unknown time zone, invalid registrations, a code already used by another organisation or another request, a reserved code, a receipt prefix that is unsafe or has no receipts to apply to, and a financial year that does not match the start month are all refused.
  • One approved request creates exactly one organisation. Running the same request again continues where it stopped, or returns the organisation it already created; running a request id again with different contents is refused.
  • Onboarding happens in steps: create the organisation, save its profile and registrations, create the Organisation administrator role, enable the chosen modules (with their roles, ledger accounts and receipt series), apply the receipt prefix, set the financial year start month and open the requested year, add the primary administrator, check readiness, activate. Each finished step is recorded; an interrupted or failed onboarding resumes after the last finished step and never repeats one.
  • A requested receipt prefix replaces the prefix of the chosen modules' receipt series for this organisation only, and only while nothing is numbered in that series yet.
  • The financial year start month is set before the requested year opens, so the year is labelled and bounded by the organisation's own start month.
  • The primary administrator's account is created, or reused if that email already has one. A reused account keeps its name and password, and is added only to the new organisation, with the Organisation administrator role there; its memberships elsewhere do not change.
  • After the contact details, address and registrations are saved in the organisation, the onboarding request no longer keeps its own copy of them.

Activation and sign-in

  • Before activation the readiness checks must pass: both names, contact email, address and time zone present; the Organisation administrator role exists with full access; the primary administrator is an active member holding it; every chosen module is enabled and its receipt series exist; the requested financial year is open.
  • Only an active organisation can be signed in to. While provisioning, after a failed provisioning, while suspended and once closed, every sign-in is refused, existing sessions stop working and the organisation's API keys are refused.
  • The primary administrator's single-use, seven-day invitation is sent only after the organisation is active. If sending fails, the failure is recorded and a retry sends it again; it never creates another organisation or administrator.
  • The organisation's sign-in page shows only its code, display name, logo and sign-in options. An unknown code and an organisation that is not active get exactly the same "not available" answer, so the page cannot be used to discover organisations. Lookups are rate-limited like sign-in.
  • On the organisation's own sign-in page nobody types an organisation code. The organisation of the last successful sign-in on the device is remembered, and the general sign-in opens that organisation's page, keeping where the person was going. "Use another organisation" forgets it and shows the general sign-in, which keeps an organisation-code field as the fallback.
  • After setting a password from an invitation or a reset link, the person is sent to their organisation's sign-in page.

Product identity

  • The product is always called OneNGO: on sign-in, in the application bar, in the installed-app name and in every browser title. Beside the OneNGO mark the application shows the organisation's display name and logo.
  • Browser titles read <screen> | OneNGO; on Home and on an organisation's sign-in page the screen is the organisation's display name.

Lifecycle

  • Only an active organisation can be suspended, and a reason is required. Suspending ends every session of the organisation at once and stops its API keys. No data is deleted.
  • Only a suspended organisation can be reactivated. Reactivation does not bring back sessions that ended or keys that were revoked; members sign in again with their valid credentials.
  • Only a suspended organisation, or one whose provisioning failed, can be closed, and a reason is required. Closing keeps all data for export and retention; a closed organisation is not reactivated.
  • Every onboarding step and every operator action is kept in the platform history with the operator's identity. Activation, suspension, reactivation and closing also appear in the organisation's own audit log, attributed to the operator.

Receipts

  • Every receipt keeps the issuer snapshot taken when it was issued. Changing the organisation's names, address, registrations or logo later never changes an issued receipt; new receipts carry the new identity.
  • A receipt's issuer snapshot cannot be changed or deleted.
  • A receipt issued before issuer snapshots were kept shows the organisation's current identity, and says so.
  • The printed receipt is headed by the issuer as kept: the legal name (or the display name without one), the display name when it differs, the address, the contact details, the registrations in force on the receipt date and the logo it was issued with.

5. User flows

Find / view

An operator asks for an organisation's status by code (or a request's status by request id) and sees its names, status, region, when its status last changed, how far onboarding got, the invitation state and the history of steps and actions. An organisation administrator opens the organisation settings to see the identity, registrations, logo and setup checklist. Every member sees the display name and logo in the application. Anyone opening /o/<code>/sign-in sees the organisation's display name and logo while it is active.

Sign in

A person opens their organisation's sign-in link (from the invitation, or remembered from their last sign-in) and sees OneNGO with the organisation's name and logo and its sign-in options, and signs in with email and password, an emailed code or an identity provider, without typing an organisation code. If the link's organisation is unknown or not active they see one neutral "not available" message and can choose "Use another organisation". The general sign-in asks for the organisation code.

Create

The operator prepares a request file with the request id, organisation code, display and legal names, contact details, address, time zone, language, hosting region, registrations, primary administrator, modules, financial year and optional receipt prefix. The operator can first check it without onboarding. Onboarding prints the organisation's id, code, status, last step and invitation state, and never the request's details, tokens or secrets. The primary administrator receives the invitation, sets a password and signs in.

Update

An organisation administrator changes the display name, legal name and default language in the organisation settings; the contact details, address and time zone; adds, corrects and removes registrations; and uploads, replaces or removes the logo. They confirm the organisation identity and that they have reviewed the receipt series; when every required item is done, setup is marked complete with who completed it.

The settings open with the setup checklist (each item done or still to do, required or optional, and the two confirmations), then the identity (organisation code and sign-in link shown read-only, legal name, contact, address, time zone), the registrations and the logo. Problems are shown on the field they concern, whether found before saving or reported when saving; a duplicate registration is shown on its number. Removing a registration asks for confirmation. A logo that is not a PNG, JPEG or WebP image of at most 1 MB is refused before it is sent. A new display name or logo shows in the application bar at once.

Other actions

The operator retries a failed or interrupted onboarding, or a failed invitation, by request id. The operator suspends an active organisation (with a reason), reactivates a suspended one, and closes a suspended one (with a reason).

7. Out of scope / non-goals

  • Self-registration by an NGO, subscription billing, and an operator web console.
  • Public organisation search, autocomplete or finding an organisation from a person's email.
  • Deleting an organisation or its data on closing; retention and export rules apply instead.
  • Changing an organisation code after onboarding.
  • A logo from another website, or a vector (SVG) logo.
  • An organisation switcher for people who belong to several organisations.
  • Remembering more than one organisation on a device.
